Operations & Monitoring

Once your team is running, most of your day-to-day control happens from the control panel. This guide covers the core operating moves: seeing status, steering the fleet, and staying reachable when a ticket needs a human.

Seeing the live status

The control panel shows you, at a glance:

  • Tickets — what's open, in progress, awaiting a human, or merged.
  • Workers — which roles are idle, busy, or assigned.
  • Pull requests — what's awaiting merge and what's merged.
  • Escalations — anything that needs a human.

This is the same machine-readable state the command-line surface exposes, rendered as a dashboard. If you prefer the command surface, the two always agree.

Steering the fleet

OpenFlows lets you control how much and what the team works on, live:

  • Pause. Let anything already in flight finish, but don't pick up new work. Useful when you want to hold the line before a release.
  • Drain. Stop taking on new tickets entirely (while finishing what's active).
  • Target. Focus the team on a specific repository, issue, or label — for example, "only work on the hotfix label right now."
  • Resume / normal. Return to business as usual.

Because the controller re-reads its steering state every cycle, these take effect on the very next cycle. No restart, no downtime.

Editing the team (the registry)

From the panel you can also edit the agent registry — how many workers each role runs, which model, which skills and tools. Changes are validated (you can't accidentally zero out a role) and take effect on the next cycle.

Recovering when something's stuck

Most problems are absorbed automatically. When they aren't, you have two levers:

  • Wait. A persistent failure is retried every cycle; many "stuck" states clear themselves shortly.
  • Reset. If a team's work is genuinely wedged and automatic recovery has already given up, reset that team back to a clean state. This returns stale or failed tickets to the start and clears the recovery counters, so the team can take a fresh pass.

What you should see

  • The new state appears in the panel's status view.
  • The team's next cycles honor the change — halting new work if paused, focusing if targeted, rescaling if you changed role counts.
  • No restart banner — the team just adjusts smoothly.

Best practices

  • Prefer pause or target over stopping the whole system when you only want to redirect work.
  • Check escalations first thing: a parked ticket is your queue of decisions that need a human.
  • Use reset sparingly and only after automatic recovery has given up — it restarts work, so it isn't free.

Wiring notifications: stay reachable

OpenFlows runs autonomously, but it is not unsupervised. When a ticket escalates to a human, you want to know promptly. Notifications turn "check the panel later" into "you were told now," and they include direct links to the workspace and pull request so you can act immediately.

OpenFlows can notify you through Slack, Discord, or WhatsApp, and they can be combined. Each channel needs its connection details supplied at setup:

  • Slack — a webhook URL for your workspace or channel.
  • Discord — a webhook URL.
  • WhatsApp — the Twilio account credentials and phone numbers (your sending number and the destination).

Notifications are engineered to be useful, not noisy: each one names the ticket, the reason, the role involved, and includes links; they are rate-limited to at most one per channel per ticket within a window; and they are fire-and-forget, so a down channel is logged and never breaks the loop. For the full behavior reference, see Notifications Reference.

Wire at least one channel from the start so you never miss an escalation. Remember that notifications are only one surface — escalations always also appear in the control panel, so nothing is lost if a channel is briefly unavailable.
